01 — The problem

Wanjiru loses 3–8% of every invoice.
And waits a week for it.

The challenge's named persona: "Small exporters in agriculture, crafts and digital services struggle to collect from international buyers without expensive intermediaries."

PERSONA

Wanjiru · FreshLeaf Exports

  • 30–60 orders / month, USD 800–6,000 each

Her week, today

What it costs

  • Hours of admin + "where is my payment?" anxiety, every week
  • At USD 30k/month, 4% leakage = USD 1,200 / month — more than this hackathon's prize, every month

02 — Why existing rails fail her

Every option trades speed, cost, or dignity.

None of them owns the exporter's actual workflow: invoice → collection → conversion → settlement → partner splits → follow-up.

SWIFT bank wires

USD 25–45 + 3–7 days

Intermediary deductions nobody can predict, paperwork, and buyers under USD 2,000 often refuse to wire at all. The bank's arrival FX rate is opaque.

PayPal · Wise · Payoneer

~5% + withdrawal holds

Kenya withdrawal friction (multi-day holds, third-party services), no M-Pesa-native settlement, no EAC mobile money, invoicing built for Western freelancers — not Murang'a aggregators.

Bank merchant acquiring

paperwork wall

Requires documentation informal SMEs don't have; onboarding takes weeks; Stripe doesn't serve Kenya-registered SMEs natively. The intermediaries the challenge names.

Generic PSP checkout pages

acceptance ≠ workflow

A pay button solves acceptance only. No AI invoicing from WhatsApp, no transparency on FX, no agent splits, no settlement ETAs, no risk/compliance trail — the exporter still does everything by hand.

The gap: a product that owns the whole exporter workflow — and rides licensed rails instead of reinventing them. That's Kusanya.

05 — The AI layer · TypeSafe Jev

Not a chatbot. Calibrated decisions inside the payment pipeline.

Jev returns typed answers with probabilities — code owns the workflow, the model supplies programmable common sense. Every judgment is logged and merchant-visible.

1 · EXTRACTION

WhatsApp → invoice

One batched call: buyer Choice, amount/currency selection from code-extracted candidates, firm-order Noul, quality Score. Dates & numbers resolved deterministically — zero hallucinated amounts.

2 · RISK COMPOSITE

Screen every invoice

Batched Nouls — sanctions language, amount anomaly vs buyer history, first-buyer, jurisdiction, name mismatch — weighted in code → pass / review / hold with reason chips + probabilities.

3 · INTENT ROUTING

Buyer replies, handled

Promise-to-pay / dispute / question / spam classified and routed into the follow-up engine; low confidence → merchant sees the raw message. No lost context, no wrong auto-replies.

4 · GUARDRAILS

AI drafts, human sends

Every drafted reminder passes an unsafe-claims Noul + per-claim citation check against ledger facts before the merchant approves it. Nothing auto-sends to a buyer.

Audit trail by design. State, criteria, probabilities and overrides persisted per judgment — the screen a Payaza compliance officer can pilot against.

Fail-safe, never fail-open. AI unavailable → manual form prefilled with regex guesses; new buyers default to review. Money flows even if Jev doesn't.

Economics that work. Batched System-One calls cost < USD 0.01 / invoice — viable at SME ticket sizes where per-token LLMs aren't.

07 — Feasibility

A pilot in months — because Payaza holds the licenses.

Kusanya is a workflow layer on regulated rails: no custody, no license of our own, no PCI scope. That's what makes "feasible within a few months" honest.

Regulatory posture

  • Payments run on Payaza's licensed infrastructure (CBN & Bank of Ghana licensed; Kenya rails live in their docs) — funds never touch our servers
  • PCI-DSS scope avoided by design: card data enters only Payaza's hosted checkout — we never see a PAN
  • KYC tiers mirror Payaza's own test/live gate: Tier 1 (phone/email + ID, small limits) → Tier 2 (KYB docs, full limits)

AML & data protection

  • Jev composite risk screening on every invoice + immutable audit trail (state, criteria, probabilities, overrides)
  • Hard holds on high-probability sanctions hits; human escalation; sender blocks populated on every payout per Payaza AML fields
  • Kenya DPA 2019 posture: PII minimized, encrypted, exportable/deletable; demo fixtures are synthetic personas only

Engineering reliability

  • Next.js 15 + TypeScript strict + shadcn/ui + Postgres/Drizzle — one Vercel deploy, webhook URL live day one
  • Webhook is truth: HMAC-SHA512-verified events drive a typed state machine; client callbacks are hints; polling + cron reconciliation cover webhook gaps
  • Idempotency everywhere: unique references, dedupe indexes, no automatic double-payouts; integer minor-unit money math

Demo-day proof

  • Built against Payaza sandbox from day one: test cards (3DS + non-3DS), momo test-funding endpoint, test payment links
  • Demo Mode: recorded real sandbox payloads replayed through the same pipeline — the 4-minute judge demo never depends on venue Wi-Fi
  • Playwright demo-path regression suite must stay green to ship
